Life OS — one place to run your life, run by an agent

Start Here

A short introduction: what this is and why it exists. For commands, formats, and the day-to-day mechanics, this page hands off to the Handbook below.

The idea: one place to run your whole life — what you're doing, where you're heading, how it's going, what you know — instead of databases, templates and apps you have to build and keep tidy yourself. Most productivity setups make you the maintainer, and the system becomes one more project that gets dropped. Here an AI agent does the running: you're interviewed once, and from then on it plans, triages, keeps the record and compiles what you learn, while you show up, report honestly and choose between the options it offers.

Projects get dropped. Not from a lack of ideas — from the accumulation of small, unglamorous bookkeeping: what the plan was, whether yesterday actually went the way it felt like it went, whether a hunch is really a pattern or just one bad day dressed up as insight. This vault, run together with an AI agent, exists to carry that bookkeeping so the load on you doesn't compound.

Two zones, one direction. Life (Life/) is the daily operating system — calendar, journal, patterns, coaching. Library (Library/) is what you learn, compiled from what you actually read into a linked wiki (Karpathy's "LLM Wiki" pattern). Life may drop a file into Library's queue; Library never writes back into Life. Two different jobs, kept from bleeding into each other.

First step after setup: run /avatar. It is a short interview — who you are, where you're heading, what you can do — that writes the description every later plan is built on, and clears the sample person's data out of the vault (after asking). Then /roadmap turns it into phases, priorities and a first step, and the daily loop starts.

Three things worth knowing before you start. The vault ships with a made-up person's data (John) so it isn't empty on first open — /avatar clears it after asking, nothing is deleted for good, and he can be brought back. The most personal files — avatar, roadmap brief, inbox, journal, notes, patterns — are kept out of git on purpose, so pushing to a public repo doesn't publish them. And the Hub's Status tab shows facts computed by code, not by feel: how old your avatar is, how long since the last /weekly, whether the current phase is past its date. The details of all three are in the Handbook, under Vault-wide at the bottom.
