# Content of the roadmap interview

Read after step 1. "Variants" feeds step 2, sections 3–6 and 1–2 feed step 3 in that order, "Review" feeds review mode.

## Variants

**Purpose:** the user gets to choose between real alternatives instead of approving one plan.

Build each variant from the avatar:
- **What wins a collision** — the priority order in one line (obligations first, then the goal work).
- **The phases** — up to three, one line each, with the outcome each ends in (from the avatar's section 2).
- **The weekly hours split** — hours per priority, total at most the avatar's free hours (section 5; for a range, its lower end); when that number is missing, ask for it once.
- **What it gives up** — the one thing this variant deliberately does not do or delays.

**Really different means:** a different top priority, or the same work in a different order (what ships first, what waits). When free hours are few, the difference is the order and what waits, not the hour split. Two variants that differ only in wording count as one.

**Recommend one** and name the avatar line behind it — a weak *finishing* skill (section 3, working skills) or *overextending* (section 6) points to fewer parallel projects, a tight free-hours number to a slower pace, an unfinished commitment in section 7 to closing it first.

**Done when** 2–3 variants are on the table, one is recommended with its reason, and the user has answered.

## 3. Phases

**Purpose:** what the next three months end in, checkable from outside.

A table with columns `Phase | Goal | Definition of Done | Target date | Status`.
- **Detail:** at most three phases are written in full, one `active`, the others `next`. Later phases toward the 12-month outcome get one row each: a one-line goal, DoD `(defined when it becomes next)`, no date (`—`), status `later`. When the avatar does not say what follows the active phase, offer two candidate next phases and ask which is closer.
- **Definition of Done** is something a stranger could verify — a command that runs on a real input, an exam passed, a document sent, a number reached. Propose a wording from the phase goal and ask the user to correct it, rather than asking what it should be; "feel ready" and "make progress" are rewritten with them until they can be checked. Counts and roles, not personal names ("three clients", "my advisor").
- **Target date** is an absolute date; relative ones ("in ten weeks") are converted from today and confirmed once.
- A phase whose definition of done must be met *before* a hard deadline in the avatar's section 4 (an exam, a filing) and whose date falls after it is flagged and moved; recurring crunch periods are planned around, not treated as deadlines. One that ends close to a deadline gets a margin, sized from the avatar's *estimating time* level (the weaker, the wider) and named as such. A later row's date cell is `—`.

**Done when** the active and `next` phases each have a checkable DoD and a confirmed date.

## 4. Priority

**Purpose:** collisions get resolved by the list, not re-argued every week.

- List what competes for the same hours — fixed commitments from the avatar's section 4 (a job has no phase and still belongs), the active phase's goal, the outcomes of later phases and other 12-month goals from section 2, open items from section 7. Keep at most five.
- Order them by asking, for each neighbouring pair, "when A collides with B, which wins?" — the pair questions may go in one message. An item the user leaves unranked gets one follow-up with a recommended slot, or is dropped from the list. Read the resulting order back.
- **Protected time** goes below the list as its own line: the avatar's section 8, with days and hours. It is not ranked; it is never traded.

**Done when** the order is strict (no ties), read back, and confirmed, and protected time is written.

## 5. Closed decisions

**Purpose:** decisions that stay decided.

Propose two to four candidates **derived from the avatar**, each with the line it comes from:
- weak *finishing* (section 3, working skills) or *overextending* / *letting go* (section 6) → "no new project until the active phase's DoD is met";
- something dropped before (section 7) → a rule against the pattern that dropped it;
- a non-negotiable (section 8) that section 4's *protected time* does not already cover → "kept at N per week, not cut for a project".

All candidates go in one message, one line each with the avatar line behind it; the user answers each separately — yes, rewrite, or no. A candidate with no line in the avatar behind it is not proposed, and one that only repeats protected time is dropped.

**Done when** every candidate has an answer; zero approved is a valid result.

## 6. Now

**Purpose:** what `/evening` and `/weekly` read every day and every week. The labels are fixed: **Current step**, **Weekly targets**, **Day off**.

- **Current step** — one line per active project: the next concrete, checkable step (not "work on X"): it names what it produces, and a day when the date matters. You propose the wording; the user corrects. When the avatar suggests no step, offer two candidates and ask which fits.
- **Weekly targets** — core hours per week (from the chosen variant, at most the free hours — the lower end of a range), the number of days the daily minimum should be met (the daily minimum is the smallest checkable piece of a block that still counts the day, as the collaboration rules define it), and any protected recurring item that is counted (e.g. gym N×).
- **Day off** — how much room the priority project gets on a day without fixed commitments (read those days off the avatar's section 4), and where it sits in the day (from the avatar's best hours, section 5). Those hours are part of the weekly targets, not extra.

**Done when** each active project has a current step, the three numbers are written, and the day-off rule names a time of day.

## 1. Context and 2. Collaboration rules (written last)

**Context** — two to four short bullets: who, the fixed schedule, the dead time, the one constraint that shapes planning most. It points to `avatar.md` for the rest; it does not copy it.

**Collaboration rules** — after the template's pointer to `COLLABORATION.md`, the three standard bullets (a checkable minimum per block; the priority order in section 4 applies; a new idea goes to `backlog.md`, not into the plan), plus at most two from the avatar's section 9 that change how planning is done.

**Done when** both fit in a few lines and add nothing that section 3–6 already say.

## Review

Steps 1–7 of review mode in `SKILL.md`; details:

- **DoD check.** One criterion at a time, in the order written; a sentence with several conditions ("sent … and each answered") is split into them and each is checked. "Met" needs a fact the user can name (the command's output, the score, the sent document). A partial fact is stated as counts ("two of three answered"). "Mostly" is not met: name what is missing.
- **Not met.** Offer three options with a recommendation and its cost: extend the date (to when; check the next phase's date and say whether it must move or has slack), cut the scope (which criterion goes, so the DoD stays checkable), or split the phase (what ships now, what becomes the next phase).
- **Backlog.** Each item gets one of three answers. *Pull* means it becomes a line in the next phase's goal or DoD. *Keep* stays untouched. *Drop* is removed from the backlog file. Confirm before writing, and only what was confirmed.
- **Next phase.** Expand the `next` or `later` one-liner into a full row; the avatar's constraints apply again (free hours, protected time). Section 6 is rewritten for it, and the closed phase's name is cleared out of sections 4 and 5.
- **Closed decisions.** Candidates come from what this phase showed — a rule that was broken and hurt, a limit that turned out real, or a way of handling something that worked and should repeat — each with its evidence.
- **The result.** Show before and after for each changed section, then write.
